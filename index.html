// the problem

Why Perf Sentinel?

I/O anti-patterns hit any app, monolith or microservices. In distributed systems one request cascades across several services, so the waste only shows up when you look at the whole trace.

Existing tools each cover a slice: Hypersistence is JPA-only, Datadog and New Relic are heavy proprietary agents, Sentry is tied to its SDK. Perf Sentinel detects these anti-patterns at the protocol level, on your own infrastructure, as a CI gate or as an OTLP daemon.

Perf Sentinel reads the traces your app already emits (SQL, HTTP, broker publishes), whatever the language or ORM: it sees the queries they generate, without needing to know JPA or EF Core.

Each avoidable I/O is also priced in energy and CO₂, bottom-up and attributable to the code.

Read this first
Perf Sentinel v0.26.2 Perf Sentinel Hub v0.3.8

Prerequisite: your services send OpenTelemetry traces of their database, HTTP and messaging calls, or Datadog traces through an OpenTelemetry Collector.

Check your tracing first: a database or HTTP call recorded without its query or URL is skipped. A thin report can mean a clean app or thin instrumentation.

What it is not: a full APM, a continuous profiler, nor (for now) a standalone regulatory carbon accounting platform. See the comparison.

Maturity: beta, pre-1.0, so commands and configuration can still change. In production with one team since June 2026.

Plus four structural patterns:

Excessive fanout

A single operation (span) has more than 20 child calls (SQL, HTTP…), parallel or not.

Chatty service

A single user request makes more than 15 outbound HTTP calls, whatever their target.

Pool saturation

At least 10 SQL queries from one service in flight at the same time within one trace, a likely sign of connection pool contention.

Serialized calls

At least 3 sibling I/O calls made one after another, on different templates, that could run in parallel.

Every finding carries: type, severity, normalized template, occurrences, source endpoint, suggestion, source location and GreenOps impact. In daemon mode, cross-trace correlation is added.

A C# Hub, for finding history

Version 10.0.401

A Perf Sentinel in daemon mode holds its findings in memory, oldest evicted first, and /api/findings answers at most 1,000 rows. That covers the current state but not the history. The Hub is a separate service that collects from every daemon and keeps what an eviction or a restart would erase, such as the date a finding first appeared.

PUSH

Daemons push, the Hub polls as a fallback

Each daemon posts its findings and retries a failed batch, and the Hub polls as a fallback.

HISTORY

Every avoidable I/O has a cost,
in time, energy and carbon

co2.total = (E × I) + M
~2× bracket, low and high bounds in every report
~2× bracket, low and high bounds in every report

Cutting N+1s and redundant calls improves response times and energy use at the same time. co2.total follows the Software Carbon Intensity v1.0 numerator (ISO/IEC 21031:2024): energy used (E) times the electricity grid carbon intensity (I), plus embodied hardware emissions (M), summed over the analyzed traces.

I/O intensity score (IIS)

An endpoint's total I/O operations divided by its number of invocations.

I/O waste ratio

Avoidable operations over total operations, the directly recoverable share.

Multi-region co2.total

SCI v1.0 numerator, automatic per-region scoring when spans carry cloud.region.

Directional estimate (~2× bracket in proxy mode, tighter with a measured energy sourcetighter with a measured energy source). The ~2× margin is enough to prioritize, compare before and after, and even to report when no measured source can be deployed: every report states its bounds. Usable as activity data for Watershed · Sweep · Greenly · Persefoni, or to support your RGESN work. It can also emit periodic public energy and carbon disclosure reports (quarterly or yearly JSON, optional Sigstore signature, hash-verifiable).

What Perf Sentinel is not

✗Not a full APM replacement. It has no RUM, no log aggregation and no alerting UI, which Datadog, New Relic and Sentry cover.
✗Not a continuous profiler. It watches I/O patterns rather than CPU time or allocations, and pairs well with Grafana Pyroscope.
✗Not a monitoring platform. A daemon keeps a bounded ring of recent findings, and PerfSentinelHub adds the history across a fleet.
✗Not (yet) a standalone regulatory carbon accounting platform. Standalone CSRD or GHG Protocol Scope 2/3 reporting needs third-party verification it does not have. Scope and pairings: see the GreenOps section.
✗Not a replacement for measured energy. The I/O-to-energy model is a directional estimate, refined by Alumet, Scaphandre, Kepler, Redfish or cloud energy APIs. What software-only attribution covers: see the limitations docs.
✗Not zero-config. Protocol-level detection requires OTel instrumentation in your apps.
✗Not an IDE plugin. For in-IDE feedback on JVM/.NET code as you type, Digma offers a well-integrated JetBrains experience.

AGPL-3.0, and what it means for your code

open source

A practical, legal-team-friendly summary so nobody has to dig through the repository. Perf Sentinel is licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0.

✓
Your services stay yours

Running Perf Sentinel does not place your own services under AGPL. It is a standalone process: your applications only send it OpenTelemetry traces over the network (OTLP), a remote communication and not a compile-time link, so it creates no derivative work and imposes no licensing obligation on your code.

✓
Unmodified binaries, no obligation

The AGPL covers Perf Sentinel’s own source code. Using the official binaries or container image unmodified triggers no copyleft obligation of any kind.

The one condition: if you modify Perf Sentinel and offer the modified version to third parties over a network, article 13 requires you to make that modified source available to those users. This is a practical summary, not legal advice. Consult your legal team if in doubt.

// for your security team

What your security team will check, and where to find it

Perf Sentinel and its Hub install on your infrastructure, with no SaaS. Here are the points a security review usually covers, with what is on by default and what is left to you.

DATA

Queries normalized in memory

The engine replaces SQL values and URL identifiers with placeholders in memory, with documented exceptions. The Hub keeps these findings 180 days by default, in a SQLite database on your side.

FLOWS

No telemetry

The engine only reaches what you configure, such as an energy measurement source. The Hub checks GitHub once a day for the latest version, and one setting turns it off.

ACCESS

What is open, and how to close it

The daemon listens on 127.0.0.1 by default, TLS optional. Its reads are not authenticated: a NetworkPolicy or your ingress filters them. The Hub requires a key per source and can put its UI behind your SSO.

CODE AND IMAGE

Rust without unsafe, rootless images

The engine’s core forbids unsafe code at compile time. Both images run without root, FROM scratch for the engine and chiseled for the Hub, read-only in their Helm charts.

SUPPLY CHAIN

Signed tags, traceable binaries

Signed release tags, build provenance attested through Sigstore and an SPDX SBOM with every version. Actions pinned by SHA, dependencies audited daily.

REPORTING

Private reporting, published response times

Vulnerabilities are reported privately through GitHub. Each repository publishes its response times and the versions that receive fixes.
